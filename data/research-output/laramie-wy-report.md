**Other Interesting Spots & Hidden Gems**

**1. Matthew Shepard Memorial Bench**
*   **What Makes It Worth Visiting:** Located on the University of Wyoming campus, this simple bench is the only public memorial in the state dedicated to Matthew Shepard, the university student whose 1998 murder became a turning point in the fight for LGBTQ+ rights in America. It is a powerful, understated, and essential place for quiet reflection on a pivotal moment in modern American history.
*   **Location:** University of Wyoming Campus, just outside the Wyoming Union.
*   **Photo Challenge:** Capture the bench and its plaque in a way that conveys its quiet dignity, perhaps using a low angle during the soft light of early morning or late afternoon.
*   **Scavenger Hunt Task:** The plaque reads, "He continues to make a difference." Find the name of the foundation mentioned on the bench that works to "replace hate with understanding, compassion, and acceptance."

**2. The "Bill Nye Trail" (Self-Guided History Hunt)**
*   **What Makes It Worth Visiting:** According to university history, the famed 19th-century humorist Edgar Wilson "Bill" Nye lived in Laramie from 1876 to 1887, serving as postmaster and editor of the *Laramie Boomerang* newspaper. While no specific building is highlighted in the sources, this provides a unique theme for exploring the historic downtown area. Wander the streets looking for architectural details and historical markers from this period, imagining the town through the eyes of one of America's great wits.
*   **Location:** Focus on the historic buildings in the downtown area adjacent to the university campus.
*   **Photo Challenge:** Find a building facade that looks like it could have existed in the 1880s. Photograph it in black and white to emphasize its historic textures and character.
*   **Scavenger Hunt Task:** Locate any historical marker or building cornerstone in the downtown area with a date between 1876 and 1887.

**3. University of Wyoming Campus**
*   **What Makes It Worth Visiting:** The University of Wyoming is the cultural and intellectual hub of Laramie. Its sprawling, park-like campus is perfect for an aimless walk. Discover the mix of classic sandstone buildings and modern structures, find quiet quads perfect for reading, and observe the daily flow of student life. It’s a landscape of public art, hidden gardens, and academic atmosphere.
*   **Location:** The area bordered roughly by E Grand Ave, N 9th St, E Lewis St, and N 22nd St.
*   **Photo Challenge:** Find the most compelling piece of public art or sculpture on campus. Photograph it from an unusual perspective—through the leaves of a tree, reflected in a window, or from ground level looking up.
*   **Scavenger Hunt Task:** The heart of the original campus is a large, open lawn known as Prexy's Pasture. Walk its perimeter and count how many major academic buildings directly face this central green space.

**4. Laramie's City Parks**
*   **What Makes It Worth Visiting:** The city boasts 14 parks, which serve as the community's backyards. These green spaces, often nestled in residential areas near the university, offer an authentic glimpse into local life. Washington Park, one of the oldest, is a large, tree-filled expanse perfect for a break from walking. They are simple, un-touristy spots to relax and observe the town's unhurried pace.
*   **Location:** Washington Park is located near downtown, centered around E 18th St and E Sheridan St.
*   **Photo Challenge:** Capture a slice-of-life photo that feels uniquely Laramie—perhaps an old-fashioned park bench under a vast Wyoming sky or the contrast of fall colors against the high-plains landscape.
*   **Scavenger Hunt Task:** Find the oldest-looking tree in Washington Park. Without touching it, try to guess its species and estimate its age based on its size and character.
